About the evidence

This project uses aggregate hospital records to explain a gap between admissions and bed days. The figures describe patterns, not why individual crashes happened. Data cover 2011–2021; the age-group and calculator views use 2021.

Data and preparation

BITRE national hospitalised injury records, 2011–2021, are filtered to traffic injury and prepared in KNIME. Age-specific rates divide the BITRE counts by June 2021 age-group population from the Australian Bureau of Statistics.

How the measures are calculated

Age-group share

Pedestrian admissions in an age group ÷ all road-injury admissions in that same age group × 100.

Population rate

Pedestrian admissions in an age group ÷ its June 2021 ABS population × 100,000.

Seasonal index

A season's admissions relative to the average season for the same series. An index of 112 means 12% above its own average.

Scenario calculator

Selected group's pedestrian bed days ÷ 365 × an assumed reduction. It is arithmetic, not an estimated policy effect.

Definitions

Admission
A recorded hospital separation; it is not necessarily a unique person.
Bed day
A day of hospital care, including transfers and changes in care type.
Rate per 100,000
Admissions divided by age-group population and multiplied by 100,000. It is not adjusted for walking exposure.
Seasonal index
A season's value relative to the average season for the same series; 100 means average.

Read with care

  • Changes in admission rules in 2012 and 2017, and bed-day counting in July 2015, limit comparisons across those dates.
  • 2021 death-related fields are blank. Missing values must not be interpreted as zero.
  • The data do not contain walking exposure, collision speed, crash location or clinical severity.
  • Small cells under 20 admissions should not be displayed as individual comparisons.
